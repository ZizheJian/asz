\begin{abstract}
Scientific simulations and instruments generate massive volumes of data. Error-bounded lossy compressors reduce these data volumes while controlling reconstruction errors. Within this class, prediction-based methods such as SZ3 and QoZ are widely used because they offer high compression ratios and high throughput. They tune predictor configurations for different datasets and error bounds and can often identify effective choices within their predefined search spaces. However, larger search spaces and more accurate selection increase the chance of finding better configurations, while faster evaluation makes such spaces practical.

In this paper, we develop ASZ, a prediction-based error-bounded lossy compressor that improves the scope, accuracy, and efficiency of configuration tuning. ASZ adds quantization-bin serialization order as a tuning dimension, expands the interpolation-coefficient search space beyond that of SZ3 and QoZ, and prunes ineffective predictor families. It combines accurate, low-cost candidate evaluation with reverse-greedy search to efficiently explore the resulting joint space. ASZ stores reusable configurations in an offline plan that can be applied across related files, further amortizing its tuning cost.

Experiments on 18 three-dimensional scientific fields show that, at equal PSNR, ASZ improves the geometric-mean compression ratio by $2.51\times$ over SZ3 and $2.03\times$ over QoZ. Even when ASZ's preprocessing cost is charged to every input, its end-to-end runtime remains comparable to QoZ. These results demonstrate that systematically restructuring the configuration space can deliver substantial compression gains without sacrificing practical performance.
\end{abstract}
