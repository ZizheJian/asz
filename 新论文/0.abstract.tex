\begin{abstract}
Scientific simulations and instruments generate massive volumes of data. Error-bounded lossy compressors reduce the required storage while controlling reconstruction errors. Within this class, prediction-based methods such as SZ3 and QoZ use lightweight operations to decorrelate data, achieving high compression ratios at high throughput. They also adapt their workflows to each input and error bound to improve these ratios. However, their compression ratios can potentially be further improved while keeping total runtime practical.

In this paper, we develop ASZ by co-designing configurable compression mechanisms and their tuning workflow. ASZ introduces round-wise prediction-axis choices, variable-coefficient predictors, and selectable quantization-bin serialization orders to improve compression ratios. To efficiently explore the resulting larger search space, ASZ combines reverse-greedy search with fast evaluation of candidate configurations. ASZ records the selected configurations in a configuration plan.

We evaluate ASZ on 18 scientific fields against the high-performance compressors SZ3, QoZ, and ZFP and the high-ratio compressor SPERR. On geometric average across all evaluated error bounds, ASZ achieves compression ratios at matched PSNR that are $2.48\times$, $2.07\times$, $6.18\times$, and $1.36\times$ those of SZ3, QoZ, ZFP, and SPERR, respectively, and total-runtime speedups, including full preprocessing for every file, of $1.23\times$ over QoZ and $2.33\times$ over SPERR. The plan generated for a single file can be reused repeatedly across snapshots of the same field with little loss in compression ratio. In some cases, it can even be reused across fields or applied to files with similar characteristics but different sizes.
\end{abstract}
