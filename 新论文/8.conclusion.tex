\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

ASZ first expands what an interpolation-based error-bounded compressor can configure. It introduces variable-coefficient predictors, exposes independent choices of prediction axis and QB serialization order for each round, and organizes the selected structural decisions in a configuration plan. It then makes this expanded space practical to tune. Prediction-stencil pruning limits the candidate set, an $H_1$--PSNR proxy and reusable joint distribution accelerate coefficient selection, and reverse-greedy search generates the configuration plan. The plan can be shared across related files while coefficients remain adaptive within each block.

We evaluate ASZ on 18 scientific fields against the high-performance compressors SZ3, QoZ, and ZFP and the high-ratio compressor SPERR. At matched PSNR, ASZ's geometric-mean compression ratios are $2.48\times$, $2.07\times$, and $6.18\times$ those of SZ3, QoZ, and ZFP, respectively. Its geometric-mean compression ratio is also $1.36\times$ that of SPERR, which it outperforms on 10 fields, while providing $2.85\times$ and $4.02\times$ its compression and decompression throughput. At the middle error bound, ASZ reduces total runtime by 16.38\% relative to QoZ on average when the cost of generating a fresh plan is included. \textcolor{red}{Add the PRECIP plan-reuse result after the experiment is complete.} These experiments assess how fine-grained configuration choices improve rate--distortion performance while configuration reuse and block-specific coefficient selection control their tuning cost.
