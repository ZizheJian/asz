\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

ASZ first expands what an interpolation-based error-bounded compressor can configure. It introduces variable-coefficient predictors, exposes independent choices of prediction axis and QB serialization order for each round, and organizes the selected structural decisions in a configuration plan. It then makes this expanded space practical to tune. Prediction-stencil pruning limits the candidate set, an $H_1$--PSNR proxy and reusable joint distribution accelerate coefficient selection, and reverse-greedy search generates the configuration plan. The plan can be shared across related files while coefficients remain adaptive within each block.

We evaluate ASZ on 18 scientific fields against the high-performance compressors SZ3, QoZ, and ZFP and the high-ratio compressor SPERR. At matched PSNR, ASZ's geometric-mean compression ratios are $2.48\times$, $2.07\times$, $6.18\times$, and $1.36\times$ those of SZ3, QoZ, ZFP, and SPERR, respectively. ASZ outperforms SPERR on 10 fields while providing $2.85\times$ and $4.02\times$ its compression and decompression throughput. Across all evaluated error bounds, ASZ achieves geometric-mean total-runtime speedups of $1.23\times$ over QoZ and $2.33\times$ over SPERR, including full preprocessing for every file. The plan generated for a single file can be reused repeatedly across snapshots of the same field with little loss in compression ratio. In some cases, it can even be reused across fields or applied to files with similar characteristics but different sizes. These experiments assess how fine-grained configuration choices improve rate--distortion performance while configuration reuse and block-specific coefficient selection control their tuning cost.
