\section{ASZ Design Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

ASZ organizes multilevel interpolation as a sequence of prediction rounds. Each round predicts and quantizes a target set using values reconstructed in earlier rounds. Existing interpolation compressors constrain these rounds through a fixed or repeatedly applied prediction schedule. ASZ removes this coupling by allowing every round to select its stencil family, prediction direction, and QB serialization order independently. Let $c_r=(f_r,d_r,o_r)$ denote these three choices for round $r$, and let $C=(c_1,\ldots,c_R)$ denote the complete round-wise plan. This representation exposes configurations that cannot be expressed by a fixed stencil or a repeated direction cycle.

ASZ separates configuration decisions by their appropriate granularity. Offline preprocessing constructs $C$, which can be reused across related files. During compression, each block $b$ assigned the tunable 1D4R stencil selects one coefficient $t_{r,b}$ for round $r$ through a low-cost proxy. Fixed-coefficient 1D6R requires no such selection. The compressor then executes the recorded rounds, encodes the resulting QB stream, and stores the information needed for the decoder to replay the same schedule.

The five optimization strategies follow the dependencies of this architecture. ASZ first removes cross-round configuration coupling through its independent round-wise representation. It then constructs a compact predictor set containing tunable-coefficient 1D4R and fixed-coefficient 1D6R. Next, it makes block-wise coefficient selection inexpensive through an $H_1$--PSNR proxy and reusable statistics. QB serialization order adds an encoding choice that changes lossless compressibility without changing reconstruction. Finally, reverse-greedy search assembles the independently configurable rounds into $C$ without exhaustively enumerating their Cartesian product. Section~\ref{sec:optimization-strategies} presents these strategies in this order, and Section~\ref{sec:asz} describes how the resulting plan is executed.
